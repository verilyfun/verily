# Product

## Register

product

## One line

**Verily: do it, prove it, get paid.** Anyone can post an action that has to
happen somewhere real. Anyone can do it, with proof captured live on their
phone. Verily checks the proof, and an escrowed reward pays out when it passes.

## Users

- **Creators** post actions: a café that wants people at its opening, a coach
  who wants ten squats a day, a city club running a scavenger hunt, a
  researcher who needs photos of every bus stop on a route, friends daring
  each other.
- **Participants** do actions: for the reward, for the streak, or because the
  dare was good.
- **Reviewers** are the people who decide the hard cases: the creator for
  appeals on their own action, and Verily's scoped reviewers for flagged proof.

## What Verily is

An actions database where every action is a small promise with three parts:

1. **A place.** A pin and a radius on a map, or a code that only exists at
   the venue. Being there is part of doing it.
2. **A proof.** Video, a photo, audio, a motion pattern, or reps counted by
   the camera, captured inside the app at the moment it happens.
3. **An optional reward.** Locked in escrow when the action is published, so
   participants can see it exists before they start.

Verily is not a marketplace for gig work, a social feed, an NFT platform, or a
token launchpad. There is no trading. Crypto appears only as the unit a reward
is paid in, because an on-chain escrow is the simplest way to make "the
reward really exists" true and checkable.

## The twist

Most "proof" apps accept whatever you upload. Verily has no upload button.

- **The camera is the only way in.** No gallery picker for proof that demands
  a fresh capture. The recording is signed on the device by a hardware-backed
  key the moment it ends.
- **A live challenge.** Each attempt gets a fresh challenge from the server,
  such as a word to say or a gesture to make, so a pre-recorded or generated
  video cannot contain it.
- **A seal you cannot see.** Verified captures carry an invisible Verily seal
  and Content Credentials, so a clip that travels can still be checked.
- **Money first.** A reward is escrowed before the action goes live. Nobody
  works for a promise.

## Brand personality

Playful, honest, hand-drawn. Verily feels like a field notebook that gets
stamped when you do the thing, not a surveillance product or a gig dashboard.
It celebrates doing things in the world and speaks plainly about what proof
can and cannot show.

## Anti-references

- Gig marketplaces that turn people into task queues.
- "Quest" platforms whose actions are follows, likes, and farmable clicks.
- Proctoring and surveillance aesthetics: red boxes, face grids, threat meters.
- Deepfake-detection dashboards that present a model score as truth.
- Crypto apps where the token is the product.

## Design principles

1. **Captured, never uploaded.** Proof comes from the camera, microphone, and
   sensors at the moment of the action.
2. **The place is part of the action.** Every action says where it happens,
   and proof is checked against it.
3. **Rewards exist before work begins.** A rewarded action cannot publish
   until its escrow is funded and verified on chain.
4. **Explain outcomes, not machinery.** Participants see what happened and
   what to do next, never model names, scores, storage paths, or prompts.
5. **Private by default.** Evidence is private, short-lived, and reviewed only
   by the people who need it. Precise coordinates are never public.
6. **People decide the hard cases.** Models and rules propose; uncertain,
   valuable, or flagged proof goes to a person, and every rejection can be
   appealed once.
7. **Accounts are not people.** Verification raises the cost of faking; it is
   never a claim that an account is a unique human.
8. **Fail closed.** Missing configuration, unknown values, and verifier
   failures lead to review, never to a fake pass or an unearned payout.

## Accessibility & inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Readable contrast, text scaling, screen-reader semantics,
visible focus, reduced motion, and 44dp touch targets. Never use colour alone
for status. Design mobile portrait first for Android. Localize all
user-facing copy. Actions must not require danger, humiliation, trespass,
harassment, or disclosure of someone else's private life.
